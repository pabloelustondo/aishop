# Benchmark 03 — Manual Shelf Inspection and Review

HumanReviewerInitials: PME

## Current human process

A shelf inspector can take photographs, visually examine the shelf, and record observations in notes, messages, or a spreadsheet. Another person may later compare those notes with the photographs.

## Strengths

- Humans can apply retail knowledge and situational judgment.
- The original photograph can reveal details an automated analysis missed.
- A second reviewer can challenge the first interpretation.

## Weaknesses

- Images and findings may be stored separately or lose their relationship.
- Review status, corrections, reviewer identity, and timing may be unclear.
- Repeating identification and transcription is slow and inconsistent.
- The original observation can be overwritten by later notes.
- Comparing AI performance with human judgment requires manual reconstruction.

## AI Shop must improve

AI Shop should connect each original image, initial AI finding, and human review in one traceable record while requiring less manual organization.

## Comparison evidence

- Time from capture to review-ready record.
- Whether the original image and initial findings remain recoverable.
- Whether reviewer, decision, corrections, and timestamps are attributable.
- Number and type of disagreements between AI and human review.
- Whether a later reviewer can understand why the final disposition was reached.

The proof succeeds only if this record is easier to inspect without weakening the human reviewer's authority.
