# Anatomy of a walkthrough

## Speaker notes

This is the high-level architecture of a deck.

Slides one to five are fixed, in this order, for every sprint.

They answer why the work exists and how anyone would know it works.

From slide six the deck explains the low-level design.

Start that part with flows and states, because they show how the parts move together.

Then give each component its slide, then the records that pass between them.

Then state the design rules with their reasons.

The deck closes with proof and honesty.

Measured results show what actually happened on a named host and date.

The weak-spots slide tells the reviewer where to look hardest.

The runbook covers anything a person must do by hand, such as a phone session.

The fine-print index is last. It is a backstop, not something to present.

A small sprint needs fewer slides in the middle. It never drops the opening or the closing.
