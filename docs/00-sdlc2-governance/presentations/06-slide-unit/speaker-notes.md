# The slide unit

## Speaker notes

Every slide is a folder with exactly two files.

One is the visual. Here it is called slide.md; an SVG or another format is equally valid.

The other is speaker-notes.md, at most 50 physical lines.

The visual carries one central meaning and must work on a screen without the notes.

Choose the form that fits: a diagram for structure or behaviour, a table for exact comparison, text when text is clearest.

Visuals clarify. They do not decorate.

Write the notes to be spoken: short sentences, one thought each, plain words.

The notes explain what the visual means and why.

End every notes file with a section called Fine print.

Fine print links to the detail: a source file, a test, a log, a specification, a decision record.

A reader who never clicks must still come away with the right understanding.

If a slide only makes sense after reading its notes, the visual is too weak.
